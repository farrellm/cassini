# bronstein-volume-2/

Sources for the integration of **algebraic** functions — the subject of the second volume of
Bronstein's *Symbolic Integration*, which was planned and never published.

[`what-volume-2-would-have-covered.md`](./what-volume-2-would-have-covered.md) is the document to
read: what is known about Volume II, a reconstruction of its contents, and every source for the
material, held or not. This file is only the per-file inventory.
[`missing-resources.md`](./missing-resources.md) details what is cited and not held: what each is
for, what its absence costs, where to look, and which to get first.

This directory sits beside `papers/`, not inside it, because it is organised around one question
rather than one kind of source. See [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) for the corpus rules and
[`../downloaded-references-summary.md`](../downloaded-references-summary.md) §9 for the index rows.

**`DESIGN.md` §6.2 puts the algebraic case out of scope.** Nothing here changes that; this is the
record of what the decision is a decision *about*.

| `bronstein1998_symbolic_integration_tutorial.pdf` | **Start here.** Bronstein's *Symbolic Integration Tutorial*, 35 pp. — title page: "ISSAC'98, Rostock (August 1998) and Differential Algebra Workshop, Rutgers November 2000". §2 (*Algebraic Functions*: Hermite reduction, simple radical extensions, Liouville's theorem, the integral part, the logarithmic part) and §3.8–§3.9 (the algebraic logarithmic and exponential cases) are the nearest thing to an outline of Volume II in his own words. Statements and worked examples; proofs are deferred to its references. **Identity caveat:** this is a later revision, not the 1998 handout — the PDF was created in January 2005 and its reference [4] reads "1997. 2nd Ed., 2004". Fetched from a course page at Kobe University, not from the author's INRIA page, which is the link the Axiom bibliography gives. The same text, as LaTeX, is the *Integration* chapter of `../papers/textbooks/axiom_bookvol10-1_algebra_theory.pamphlet`. |
| `trager1984_integration_of_algebraic_functions.pdf` | **The algorithm itself.** Barry Marshall Trager, *Integration of Algebraic Functions*, PhD thesis, MIT Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, September 1984; supervisors Joel Moses and Richard Zippel; 83 leaves. Seven chapters — integral bases, absolute irreducibility, the rational part of the integral, log terms and divisors, principal divisors and points of finite order — and an appendix on simple radical extensions. **Image-only scan, no text layer:** `pdftotext` returns nothing. An OCR sidecar, `trager1984_integration_of_algebraic_functions.txt`, sits beside it; its prose is usable and its formulas are not, so read mathematics from the page images. MIT's scan (its PDF metadata is the scanning software's, not the thesis's). |
| `davenport1981_on_the_integration_of_algebraic_functions.pdf` | James Harold Davenport, *On the Integration of Algebraic Functions*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 102, Springer 1981, ISBN 3-540-10290-6; 199 PDF pages. The earlier, Puiseux-expansion algorithm, implemented in REDUCE-2 for square roots only ("the program deals only with square roots"). Read chs. 5–8 for the torsion-divisor problem at length — parameters, elliptic curves, bounds over number fields — and the appendices for worked examples with timings. **Text-layer caveats:** a scan with an OCR layer; body text is sound, some runs are letter-spaced ("i n t e g r a l"), and the contents page extracts out of order, so find chapters by grepping the body. |
| `bronstein1990_integration_of_elementary_functions.pdf` | **The mixed algebraic–transcendental case.** Bronstein, "On the Integration of Elementary Functions" (the article's own heading reads "Integration of Elementary Functions"), *J. Symbolic Computation* 9(2):117–173, 57 pp. Per its first-page footnote, a revised version of his dissertation (Mathematics, U.C. Berkeley, May 1987). Three parts: algebraic preliminaries, the integration problem, Risch's associated equation. Read the Conclusions for what was still open in 1990. **Text-layer defect:** publisher OCR — the byline extracts as "~[ANUEL B RONSTFJN", scattered runs are letter-spaced ("t r a n s c e n d e n t a l"), and formulas are noise. Greps under-count; despace first. |
| `bronstein1998_lazy_hermite_reduction.pdf` | Bronstein, *The Lazy Hermite Reduction*, INRIA Rapport de recherche n° 3562, November 1998. "13 pages" on its own cover; the HAL file is 17 with HAL's cover sheet and the INRIA front matter. Hermite reduction for algebraic functions with no precomputed integral basis or primitive element, stated over a monomial extension `k(t)`. The proofs the tutorial omits. **Text-layer trap:** HAL's cover sheet extracts cleanly and everything after it extracts as symbol garbage, so a text-layer check passes and every grep of the body fails. Grep the OCR sidecar, `bronstein1998_lazy_hermite_reduction.txt`. |
| `risch1970_solution_of_integration_in_finite_terms.pdf` | Risch, "The Solution of the Problem of Integration in Finite Terms", *Bulletin of the AMS* 76:605–608, 4 pp. The announcement of the full result. Held for the statement that the problem "reduces to the problem of bounding torsion on the Jacobian variety of an algebraic curve" and for the two-good-reductions bound. A sketch — "A complete account is planned for a future publication." Clean text layer; formulas extract poorly. |
| `bronstein1991_risch_differential_equation_algebraic_curve.pdf` | Bronstein, "The Risch Differential Equation on an Algebraic Curve", *Proceedings of ISSAC'91*, pp. 241–246, 6 pp. "A new rational algorithm for solving Risch differential equations over algebraic curves" — the piece Bronstein 1990 names as missing. In the general mixed case it finds only the denominator of a solution. **Text-layer trap:** two columns extracted interleaved, about one and a half words per line — single words grep, phrases never do. Use `pdftotext -layout` and read. |
| `bertrand1995_hyperelliptic_integral_jacobian.pdf` | Bertrand, "Computing a Hyperelliptic Integral using Arithmetic in the Jacobian of the Curve", *AAECC* 6:275–298 (1995), 24 pp. Trager's algorithm specialised to `y² = f(x)` with Cantor's Jacobian arithmetic for the divisor steps; implemented and compared against the general algorithm. Publisher-OCR text layer, sound for prose. |
| `vanhoeij1994_integral_basis_algebraic_function_field.pdf` | van Hoeij, "An Algorithm for Computing an Integral Basis in an Algebraic Function Field", *J. Symbolic Computation* 18:353–363 (1994), 11 pp. — the article prints 353–363; Bronstein's tutorial cites 353–364. Integral bases by Puiseux expansions, "a variant on the Coates' algorithm"; the abstract says Trager's is the method "used by Maple 5.2 and AXIOM". **Image-only scan, no text layer;** OCR sidecar `vanhoeij1994_integral_basis_algebraic_function_field.txt` beside it. |
| `trager1979_integration_of_simple_radical_extensions.pdf` | Trager, "Integration of Simple Radical Extensions", EUROSAM'79, LNCS 72, pp. 408–414, 7 pp. The unnested-radical case by a generalised Risch algorithm; the thesis's Appendix A has the same title. **Noisy OCR text layer** — the byline extracts as "B=rry M. Tr=g~r~" and words are letter-spaced throughout; greps are unreliable. |
| `davenport1979_algorithms_integration_algebraic_functions.pdf` | Davenport, "Algorithms for the Integration of Algebraic Functions", EUROSAM'79, LNCS 72, pp. 415–425, 11 pp. The precursor of the 1981 monograph, which supersedes it; held because Blake cites it (as "Integration of algebraic functions"). Typewritten; the text layer is sound. |
| `hardy1916_integration_of_functions_of_a_single_variable.pdf` | G. H. Hardy, *The Integration of Functions of a Single Variable*, Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics and Mathematical Physics No. 2. **The second edition of 1916 in a 1966 reprint** ("by the late G. H. Hardy"), not a 1916 printing; an Internet Archive scan of Cornell's copy, 84 PDF pages. Chapter V is the integration of algebraical functions; the conjecture everyone quotes is there — "there is reason to suppose that no such method can be given". OCR text layer: prose is good, formulas are noise, and the first pages are library stamps. |
| `abramov2006_in_memory_of_manuel_bronstein.pdf` | S. A. Abramov, "In Memory of Manuel Bronstein", *Programming and Computer Software* 32(1):56–58 (2006), 3 pp.; a translation of the Russian original in *Programmirovanie*. **The source for what became of Volume II** — "The second volume remained uncompleted" — and for the date, place and cause of his death. Clean text layer. |
| `davenport1984_integration_algorithmique_courbe_algebrique.pdf` | Davenport, "Intégration algorithmique des fonctions élémentairement transcendantes sur une courbe algébrique", *Annales de l'Institut Fourier* **34**(2):271–276 (1984); NUMDAM's file, 7 pp. with its cover sheet. In French. Solves the integration and Risch-equation problems over `K(x, y, θ₁, …, θₙ)` with `y` algebraic over `K(x)` and each `θᵢ` elementarily transcendental — transcendental extensions on top of one algebraic curve — and says the general case "me semble être non résolu". Bronstein's reference list prints the volume as 84; it is 34. Clean text layer, formulas apart. |
| `chevalley1951_algebraic_functions_of_one_variable.pdf` | Claude Chevalley, *Introduction to the Theory of Algebraic Functions of One Variable*, Mathematical Surveys VI, American Mathematical Society, 1951; 197 PDF pages, an Internet Archive scan. **The background Bronstein 1990 assumes** ("the contents of (Chevalley 1951, chapter I)") — chapter I is *Places and Divisors*, II *The Theorem of Riemann-Roch*. Bronstein cites it by a shortened title. OCR text layer: prose greps, with scattered misreadings ("Divisobs", "Eoch"); the copyright line extracts as "1961". 21.5 MB. |
| `bliss1933_algebraic_functions.pdf` | Gilbert Ames Bliss, *Algebraic Functions*, AMS Colloquium Publications XVI, 1933, in the 1947 photo-lithoprint; 229 pages. Risch 1970 cites the Dover 1966 reprint and points to chapter III, which here is *Rational Functions* — divisors and their bases, multiples of a divisor, Riemann–Roch, "Rational functions with prescribed poles". **Converted from DjVu:** the PDF is image-only, and `bliss1933_algebraic_functions.txt` beside it is the DjVu's own text layer (not a tesseract run), which is good. 16.4 MB. |
| `stichtenoth2009_algebraic_function_fields_and_codes.pdf` | Henning Stichtenoth, *Algebraic Function Fields and Codes*, **second edition**, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 254, Springer 2009, ISBN 978-3-540-76877-7; 363 pp. The modern textbook for the function-field background. **Edition caveat:** Axiom vol. 10.1 cites the 1993 first edition; cite this file as the 2009 edition. Born-digital, clean text layer. |
| `fricas_wiki_risch_implementation_status_2022_wayback.html` | The FriCAS wiki page *RischImplementationStatus*, as last edited 2022/06/28; a Wayback Machine snapshot of 2022-12-07 per the note it was supplied with (the live wiki no longer resolves, per the same note). **The authoritative statement of what the most complete open implementation does not do**: "a large part of the Risch algorithm dealing with algebraic extensions remains unimplemented"; the logarithmic part is found "only in the purely algebraic case", and there with "restrictions on the Trager resultant"; the Risch differential equation solver "can only handle the purely algebraic case". Read with `sed -e 's/<[^>]*>/ /g'`; the body text is near the end, after the wiki's scripts and navigation. |
| `miller2012_integration_logarithmic_part.pdf` | Brian L. Miller, *On the Integration of Elementary Functions: Computing the Logarithmic Part*, PhD dissertation, Texas Tech University, May 2012 (advisor Lourdes Juan); 49 pp. Per its abstract: an algorithm for the logarithmic part of the integral of an algebraic function, then one for "a function lying in a transcendental elementary extension", justified with Gröbner bases. Clean text layer. |
| `combot2021_elementary_integration_superelliptic.pdf` | Thierry Combot, "Elementary Integration of Superelliptic Integrals", ISSAC '21, 8 pp. The elementary-integration problem for `∫ P/(Q·S^{1/k}) dx`, solved "generically" with a stated complexity bound in the genus. The `y^k = S(x)` case, as Bertrand is the `y² = f(x)` case. Born-digital; mathematical italics extract as Unicode maths letters, so grep prose words, not symbols. |
| `hess2002_computing_riemann_roch_spaces.pdf` | F. Hess, "Computing Riemann–Roch Spaces in Algebraic Function Fields and Related Topics", *J. Symbolic Computation* 33:425–445 (2002), 21 pp. Riemann–Roch spaces "without the Brill–Noether method of adjoints or any series expansions", with divisor reduction and computation in the divisor class group — a modern replacement for the divisor arithmetic of Trager's chs. 5–6. Clean text layer. |
| `masser_zannier2020_torsion_points_pell_integration.pdf` | Masser & Zannier, "Torsion points, Pell's equation, and integration in elementary terms", *Acta Mathematica* 225:227–312 (2020), 87 pp. Arithmetic geometry, not algorithms: when members of a *family* of algebraic integrals are elementary. Held for one statement an implementer should know — a 1981 conjecture of Davenport's on this is "false", and they prove a modified version. |
| `fulton2008_algebraic_curves.pdf` | William Fulton, *Algebraic Curves: An Introduction to Algebraic Geometry*, the author's free 2008 edition (dated January 28, 2008; "available without charge", third preface), 129 pp. The undergraduate route into the background; Davenport's monograph cites the 1969 edition beside Chevalley. Born-digital. |
| `chen_vanhoeij_kauers2016_telescoping_fuchsian_dfinite.pdf` | Chen, van Hoeij, Kauers & Koutschan, "Reduction-Based Creative Telescoping for Fuchsian D-finite Functions", arXiv:1611.07421**v1**, 20 pp. Trager's Hermite reduction adapted from algebraic to fuchsian D-finite functions; a sequel to the 2016 paper above, peripheral to integration in finite terms. **Four authors** — the file it was supplied under named only Chen and Kauers. |
| `weil1948_sur_les_courbes_algebriques_retranscription.pdf` | André Weil, *Sur les courbes algébriques et les variétés qui s'en déduisent* (1948), the companion to the volume below. **Not a scan of the Hermann printing: a LaTeX retranscription** — "Retranscription en Latex Denise Vella-Chemla, juillet 2022" on its first page — 74 pp., so its page numbers are not the original's, and it has not been compared against the original. In French. **This is where the torsion bound comes from**: part II §IV, *Application à la fonction ζ (hypothèse de Riemann)*, proves `|1 + qⁿ − νₙ| ≤ 2g·q^{n/2}` and that the numerator `P(u)` of the zeta function is `∏(1 − αᵢu)` over `2g` roots with `αᵢᾱᵢ = q` (PDF pp. 60–61). The bound `(1 + √q)^{2g}` that Bronstein's tutorial uses is not written there in that form; it is the one-line consequence for `P(1)` (our inference). Clean text layer. |
| `weil1948_varietes_abeliennes_et_courbes_algebriques.pdf` | André Weil, *Variétés abéliennes et courbes algébriques*, Actualités scientifiques et industrielles 1064, 1948, in a 1951 photographic reprint; 163 pp., in French, **132 MB**. **Not the book the tutorial cites**, which is *Courbes algébriques et variétés abéliennes*, Hermann 1971; the note supplied with the file says the 1971 volume re-issues this material, which was not checked here. Its chapters are on abelian varieties, Jacobians, their homomorphisms and the characteristic roots of an endomorphism. **Wanted for the bound behind the finite-order test, and the bound does not appear to be here:** a search of the sidecar for finite fields, the zeta function and the Riemann hypothesis found nothing. **Image-only;** a French OCR sidecar, `weil1948_varietes_abeliennes_et_courbes_algebriques.txt`, sits beside it (tesseract `-l fra` at 150 dpi, the scan being only 1000 pixels wide). Prose greps well; the contents page and formulas do not. |
| `kauers2008_algebraic_integration_logarithmic_part.pdf` | Kauers, "Integration of Algebraic Functions: A Simple Heuristic for Finding the Logarithmic Part", ISSAC 2008, 8 pp. A Gröbner-basis heuristic — by its own abstract it "does not have the feature of finding a closed form of an integral whenever there is one". Read the introduction for the state of practice: every system holds "only … a partial implementation of the complete algorithm". Author's copy from RISC, carrying the ACM copyright block. |
| `chen_kauers_koutschan2016_reduction_telescoping_algebraic.pdf` | Chen, Kauers & Koutschan, "Reduction-Based Creative Telescoping for Algebraic Functions", arXiv:1602.00424**v1**, 8 pp. Trager's Hermite reduction plus a polynomial reduction. Its subject is telescoping, but it restates the reduction half of the integration algorithm compactly and in modern notation. **The arXiv preprint.** |
| `chen_du_kauers2021_lazy_hermite_reduction.pdf` | Chen, Du & Kauers, "Lazy Hermite Reduction and Creative Telescoping for Algebraic Functions", arXiv:2102.06538**v2**, 20 pp. Bronstein's lazy Hermite reduction, sharpened with a polynomial reduction; read his own report (above) first. **The arXiv preprint.** |
| `blake2020_pseudo_elliptic_integrals.pdf` | Blake, "A Simple Method for Computing Some Pseudo-Elliptic Integrals in Terms of Elementary Functions", arXiv:2004.04910**v3**, 12 pp., marked "DRAFT version" on its first page. A substitution heuristic, not a decision procedure. The 73.4% success figure against the full algorithm is the author's own, from his randomised tests. Its reference list is a useful short bibliography of the algebraic case. |
| `abramov_bronstein_petkovsek2020_pi_sigma_difference_equations.pdf` | Abramov, Bronstein, Petkovšek & Schneider, "On Rational and Hypergeometric Solutions of Linear Ordinary Difference Equations in ΠΣ*-field extensions", arXiv:2005.04944**v2**, 59 pp. **Held for one fact,** now also given by Abramov's obituary above: the author footnote "Passed away on June 6, 2005" and the sentence giving his age as 41. Its mathematics is difference equations, not integration; do not read it for this directory's subject. |

## Provenance

The four files in the first rows above were supplied by the project owner on 2026-11-11, downloaded
in a browser; `curl` had been refused for the first and third and not pursued for the others. Their sources are inferred from the files, not observed:
the thesis is MIT's scan (DSpace handle `1721.1/15391`), the report carries HAL's cover sheet
(`inria-00073121v1`), the 1990 paper is Elsevier's PDF (PII `S0747-7171(08)80027-2` in its
metadata), and the Risch file is named for its AMS identifier (`S0002-9904-1970-12454-5`). The other
six were fetched by `curl` the same day. Davenport's monograph is a scan from the project owner's own
copy of the book, supplied the same day; its PDF metadata names a 2013 compression tool and nothing
about the book.

Seven more were supplied by the project owner on 2026-11-12 — the ISSAC'91 paper, Bertrand, van
Hoeij, the two EUROSAM'79 papers, Hardy and Abramov's obituary. Where each was downloaded from was
not observed. Hardy's first pages identify an Internet Archive scan of Cornell University Library's
copy; the others carry their publishers' marks (ACM, Springer, Academic Press, Pleiades).

Four more followed the same day, replacing files that had arrived broken: Davenport's 1984 paper
(NUMDAM's open-access file, per its cover sheet), Stichtenoth, Chevalley and Bliss. Chevalley's PDF metadata names the Internet Archive
as its creator. Bliss was supplied as DjVu and converted here with `ddjvu -format=pdf`, its text
extracted with `djvutxt`.

Eight more came later that day with an `INDEX.md` listing a source for each, **recorded here as
reported, not verified**: Miller from Texas Tech's repository (handle `2346/45299`); the FriCAS page
from the Wayback Machine; Masser & Zannier from the publisher's open-access PDF; the arXiv paper
from arXiv. The sources it gives for the rest are not recorded here. One thing in that note was checked
and is wrong: it says arXiv:1611.07421 is by Chen and Kauers alone; the paper's first page lists four
authors.

The same delivery re-supplied Chevalley, Bliss, Stichtenoth and Davenport 1984. Those were **not**
taken: the copies already held are complete, and the held Chevalley and Bliss have text where the
re-supplied ones are image-only.

Weil's *Sur les courbes algébriques* arrived on its own after those. Its source was not stated; the
file names its transcriber and date on the first page.

## Rule for this directory

`what-volume-2-would-have-covered.md` tags every claim **[held]**, **[cited]** or **[memory]**. Keep
the tags honest: when a source is obtained, open it, then promote the claims that rest on it and move
its row from §4.4 or §4.5 to §4.1 — in the same change that indexes the file.
