\chapter[Parametric Problems]{Parametric Problems and Structure Theorems}\label{ch:parametric}

The integration algorithm of Volume~I is recursive, and the recursion does
not call integration alone. To integrate in $k(t)$ it needs, in $k$, the
solution of Risch differential equations with undetermined constants on the
right, integration up to a constant multiple of a given logarithmic
derivative, and the decision whether a given element is a logarithmic
derivative up to an integer factor; \VolI{Chapter~7} treats these as the
parametric problems. And before any of it can start, one must know that each
new logarithm or exponential is a monomial over the field built so far,
which is the business of the structure theorems of \VolI{Chapter~9}.

The mixed case of Chapters~\ref{ch:elementary} and~\ref{ch:risch-equation}
calls for the same problems with $k$ replaced by a field that contains
algebraic functions. This chapter records what is known about them there.
It is short, because little is: we have found no source that treats these
problems over a curve as problems in their own right, and where the sources
solve them at all it is inside the proof of something else.

\section{Where the parametric problems arise}\label{sec:param-arise}

Each has appeared already.

\paragraph{The parametric Risch equation.} $Dz + f z = \sum_i c_i g_i$, to be
solved for $z$ in the field and constants $c_i$. On a curve this is the
equation of Chapter~\ref{ch:risch-equation} in the form Risch states it: his
theorem is formulated for the parametric equation from the start, because
the recursion needs it~\cite[Theorem~1(b)]{raab2022}. So does Bronstein's
equation $(R_1)$~\cite[Part~3]{bronstein1990}. What
Section~\ref{sec:rde-solving} says about the equation applies to the
parametric form, with the same reliance on expansions.

\paragraph{Limited integration.}\index{limited integration} Given $f$ and $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_m$,
find $v$ in the field and constants $d_j$ with $f = Dv + \sum_j d_j\,
D\gamma_j/\gamma_j$. This is~\eqref{eq:tau-infinity}, with one $\gamma$: the
residue at an infinite place in the logarithmic case is defined by solving
it in $\kbar$. It is also the problem behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:rde-special}(ii). Risch's proof treats it as a ``simpler
variant'' of the integration problem, in which some of the logarithms are
prescribed, and carries it through the induction alongside the main
statement~\cite[p.~209 and p.~211]{raab2022}.

\paragraph{The parametric logarithmic derivative problem.}\index{parametric logarithmic derivative problem} Given $f$ and
$\eta$, decide whether $f = -\alpha\, D\eta - Dc/c$ for an integer $\alpha$
and an element $c$ of the field, and find them. This is the cancellation case
of Theorem~\ref{thm:rde-special}(i), and Bronstein's definition of the
quantity $\mu$ there is exactly this problem, of which he says only that
``it can be computed by integrating''~\cite[Definition~3.2]{bronstein1990}:
one integrates $f$ and inspects the form of the answer. That is an
algorithm only to the extent that integration in the field is one, and it
needs the integral in a normal form in which the question can be read off.
\VolI{\S7.3} gives a direct method for a field of the form $k(t)$, says of
it that it ``is not a complete algorithm'', and refers to the structure
theorems as ``the only known complete solution''; over a curve we know of no
direct method at all.

\section{What is known over a curve}\label{sec:param-known}

For a tower of \emph{transcendental} monomials the parametric problems are
treated in full, with the reservation just quoted for the third:
\VolI{Chapter~7}, and, with parameters allowed in the
integrand and the constants, Raab's thesis~\cite{raab2012}, whose third
chapter covers primitive, hyperexponential and some nonlinear extensions.
Neither treats an algebraic extension of the tower. Of Raab's thesis we have
read the table of contents and searched the text: every occurrence of
``algebraic extension'' that we found is of the field of constants or of
coefficients.

For a curve over a field of constants, $E$ finite over $K(x)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Risch equation is covered by Bronstein's rational algorithm,
  according to its abstract (Section~\ref{sec:rde-rational}); whether the
  paper treats the parametric form we have not checked;
\item limited integration is a variant of \seealg{IntegrateAlgebraic}: the
  prescribed logarithms $\gamma_j$ contribute known residue divisors, and
  the question becomes whether the residues of $f\,dx$ lie in the span of
  those of the $D\gamma_j/\gamma_j$, followed by a test for the algebraic
  part as in Chapter~\ref{ch:algebraic-part}. No source that we hold states
  this as an algorithm, and we have not worked it out;
\item the parametric logarithmic derivative problem asks whether a divisor
  determined by the residues of $f\,dx$, combined with integer multiples of
  that of $\eta$, is principal. This is a question about a subgroup of the
  divisor class group generated by two classes, and it is not answered by
  the test of Chapter~\ref{ch:finite-order}, which concerns one class.
\end{itemize}

The state of implementation says the same. The status page of FriCAS, the
maintained descendant of Bronstein's Axiom
integrator~\cite{fricas2022}, reports: ``The solver for the Risch
differential equation can only handle the purely algebraic case. The
logarithmic derivative problem is unimplemented in the algebraic case.''

\begin{openproblem}
An algorithm for the parametric logarithmic derivative problem, and for
limited integration, in a finite algebraic extension of $k(t)$, stated and
proved as such.
\end{openproblem}

We do not know whether this is open in the sense of being unsolved or only
in the sense of being unwritten. Risch's theorem implies that both are
decidable, since both are instances of asking whether an integral has a
prescribed form; what is missing is an algorithm in the sense of this book.

\section{Structure theorems}\label{sec:param-structure}

Before $t = \log \eta$ or $t = \exp \eta$ is adjoined to a field $k$, one
must know that it is a monomial over $k$ with no new constants: otherwise
it is algebraic over $k$, or differs from an element of $k$ by a constant,
and the theory of Part~III does not apply to it. The criteria are classical:
$\log \eta$ fails to be a monomial exactly when $D\eta/\eta$ is the
derivative of an element of $k$, and $\exp \eta$ fails exactly when $D\eta$
is $1/N$ times the logarithmic derivative of an element of $k$ for some
nonzero integer $N$ \VolI{Theorems~5.1.1 and~5.1.2}. Deciding them by the
definitions means solving, in $k$, an integration problem and a parametric
logarithmic derivative problem, and Risch notes at the end of his report
that these are ``simple variants'' of his main theorem and that his proof
shows how to test for them~\cite[p.~219]{raab2022}.

The structure theorems avoid the recursion. In a tower of logarithms,
exponentials and algebraic elements over the constants, they characterise
the algebraic dependence of a new logarithm or exponential on the previous
ones by the solvability of a system of linear equations over $\Q$, whose
unknowns are the multiplicities with which the arguments of the earlier
logarithms and exponentials enter. The theorem of Risch for elementary
extensions, and that of Rothstein and Caviness for Liouvillian ones, are in
\VolI{Chapter~9}, and they are stated there for towers that may contain
algebraic elements. Bronstein's conclusions mention the same result as the
way to test whether a given logarithm or exponential is a monomial
\cite[Conclusions]{bronstein1990}.

So this part of the mixed case is not missing: Volume~I has it. What the
structure theorems give is a criterion in terms of the generators of the
tower. They decide whether the new element is a monomial; they do not
solve the parametric problems of Section~\ref{sec:param-arise} for an
arbitrary element of the field.

\begin{exercise}
Let $k = \Q(x, \sqrt{x})$. Decide directly whether $\log(x + \sqrt x)$ and
$\exp(\frac12 \log x)$ are monomials over $k$ with no new constants.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
Formulate limited integration for $E$ finite over $K(x)$ in terms of residue
divisors, and say which steps of \seealg{IntegrateAlgebraic} change.
\end{exercise}
