\chapter{The Risch Differential Equation on a Curve}\label{ch:risch-equation}

Integrating exponentials leads to first-order linear differential equations.
In Volume~I the integral of $a\,e^{\eta}$ is $z\,e^{\eta}$ with $Dz + (D\eta)\,z
= a$, and \VolI{Chapter~6} solves such equations in $k(t)$. In
Chapter~\ref{ch:elementary} the same happened on a curve: the reduction at a
special place asked for solutions of the system~\eqref{eq:Sexp}, which is a
differential equation of this kind for an element of an algebraic extension.
This chapter is about the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risch}
  Dz + f\,z = g, \qquad f, g \in E ,
\end{equation}
to be solved for $z \in E$, where $E = k(t)[y]/(F)$ is as in
Chapter~\ref{ch:monomial}; and about the parametric form in which $g =
\sum_i c_i g_i$ with given $g_i \in E$ and unknown constants $c_i$, which is
what the recursion needs.

What is known is less than for integration. Risch's method solves the
equation by expansions at every pole; Bronstein's rational algorithm of 1991
is complete for a curve over constants; and for the mixed case the rational
algorithm stops at the denominator. We prove the bound at the normal places,
state the rest with its sources, treat the diagonal case completely, and say
what is open.

\section{The order of a solution at a normal place}\label{sec:rde-normal}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rde-normal}\standing{proved}{}
Let $z \in E$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:risch}, and let $P$ be a normal place with
ramification index $r$. Put $\alpha = \ord{P}(z)$, $\beta = \ord{P}(f)$ and
$\gamma = \ord{P}(g)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If $\beta < -r$, then $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma - \beta)$.
\item If $\beta > -r$, then $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma + r)$.
\item If $\beta = -r$, then either $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma + r)$, or
  $\tau_P(f)$ is a positive integer and $\alpha = -\tau_P(f)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\alpha < 0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:order-derivative}(i), $\ord{P}(Dz) =
\alpha - r$, and $\ord{P}(f z) = \alpha + \beta$.

(i) If $\beta < -r$ the two orders differ, the smaller is $\alpha + \beta$,
and it must equal $\ord{P}(Dz + fz) = \gamma$. So $\alpha = \gamma - \beta$.

(ii) If $\beta > -r$ the smaller is $\alpha - r = \gamma$, so $\alpha = \gamma
+ r$.

(iii) If $\beta = -r$, both terms have order $\alpha - r$. Either their
leading terms do not cancel, and $\alpha - r = \gamma$; or they cancel. In
the notation of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tau}, let $z = c\,\pi^\alpha +
\dots$ and $f = a\,\pi^{-r} + \dots$. The leading term of $Dz$ is $\alpha\,c\,
\pi^{\alpha - 1}\,\Delta\pi = (\alpha\,c\,\epsilon / r)\,\pi^{\alpha - r}$, and
that of $f z$ is $a\,c\,\pi^{\alpha - r}$. They cancel exactly when $\alpha =
-r a/\epsilon$, and $r a/\epsilon$ is the value at $P$ of $r f p/Dp$, which is
$\tau_P(f)$.
\end{proof}

This is Proposition~3.3 of~\cite{bronstein1990}, and for $E = k(t)$ it is
the bound of \VolI{\S6.1}. Case~(iii) is the first appearance of
\emph{cancellation}: the equation itself does not bound the pole, but it
names the only pole it permits, which is computed from $f$. The values
of $\tau_P(f)/r_P$ are roots of the polynomial $R$
of~\eqref{eq:mixed-resultant} formed for $f$, so the candidates are found
among its positive rational roots.

The same argument, with $\sum_i c_i g_i$ in place of $g$ and $\gamma$ the
least of the orders of the $g_i$, gives the same bounds for the parametric
equation.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:rde-denominator}\standing{proved}{}
Let $w$ be an integral basis of $E$ over $k(t)$. There is a $d \in k[t]$,
computable from $f$ and $g$ and with all its irreducible factors normal, such
that for every solution $z \in E$ of~\eqref{eq:risch}, $d\,z$ has no pole at
any normal place.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At a normal place where neither $f$ nor $g$ has a pole, $\beta \ge 0 > -r$
and $\gamma \ge 0$, so $\alpha \ge 0$ by~(ii). The remaining normal places
lie over the finitely many normal irreducible factors $p$ of the
denominators of $f$ and $g$ with respect to $w$. For each such $p$, the
theorem gives a lower bound $-n_P$ for $\alpha$ at each place $P$ over $p$,
and $d = \prod_p p^{N_p}$ with $N_p \ge n_P / r_P$ for all $P$ over $p$ has
the property.
\end{proof}

The proof factors the denominators into irreducibles and, in case~(iii),
needs the residues $\tau_P(f)$, which may be algebraic over $k$. That is not
a rational algorithm in the sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:logarithmic-part}.

\section{The special places}\label{sec:rde-special}

At the places that are not normal the order of $z$ is bounded by a different
mechanism, which requires computing in $k$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rde-special}\standing{cited}{\cite[Propositions~3.4 and~3.5]{bronstein1990}}
Let $z$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rde-normal},
for a place $P$ that is not normal.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Let $t = \exp \eta$ and let $P$ be unramified, over $t = 0$ or at
  infinity. If $\beta < 0$ then $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma - \beta)$; if
  $\beta > 0$ then $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma)$; and if $\beta = 0$ then
  either $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma)$, or the value $f(P) \in \kbar$ satisfies
  \[
    f(P) = -\epsilon\,\alpha\, D\eta - \frac{Dc}{c}
    \qquad \text{for some } c \in \kbar^*,
  \]
  where $\epsilon = 1$ over $t = 0$ and $\epsilon = -1$ at infinity, and this
  determines $\alpha$.
\item Let $t = \log \eta$ and let $P$ be an infinite place with ramification
  index $r$. Then either $\alpha \ge \min(0, \gamma - r)$, or the coefficient
  $a$ of $t^{-1}$ in the expansion of $f$ at $P$ satisfies
  \[
    a = -D\beta_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r}\, \frac{D\eta}{\eta}
    \qquad \text{for some } \beta_0 \in \kbar ,
  \]
  and this determines $\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In both cancellation cases the candidate $\alpha$ is found by a problem in
$\kbar$: in~(i), whether $\exp(\int f(P))$ is an element of $\kbar$ times a
power of $t$, and which power; in~(ii), whether $a$ is a derivative plus a
constant multiple of $D\eta/\eta$, and which multiple. These are the
problems that \VolI{Chapter~7} calls the parametric logarithmic derivative
problem and limited integration, here over $\kbar$; we return to them in
Chapter~\ref{ch:parametric}. Ramification at the special places in the
exponential case is removed beforehand by replacing $t$ by a root of it,
$\exp(\eta/N)$, as Risch does.

\section{Solving the equation}\label{sec:rde-solving}

With lower bounds for $\alpha$ at all places, Bronstein reduces to an
equation with an integral unknown~\cite[Proposition~3.7]{bronstein1990}. Let
$d \in k[t]$ be such that $Z = d\,z$ has no pole at a finite place, taking
into account the special place over $t = 0$ in the exponential case. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risch-integral}
  DZ + \Bigl( f - \frac{Dd}{d} \Bigr) Z = d\,g ,
\end{equation}
and $Z = \sum_i h_i w_i$ with $h_i \in k[t]$, where $w$ is an integral basis
normal at infinity. The bounds at the infinite places bound the degrees of
the $h_i$:
\[
  \deg(h_i) \le \min_P \frac{\ord{P}(w_i)}{r_P} - b + \deg(d),
\]
the minimum over the infinite places, $b$ being the least of $0$ and the
bounds for $\alpha$ there. The $h_i$ are then polynomials of bounded degree
with unknown coefficients in $k$, and substituting
in~\eqref{eq:risch-integral} and comparing expansions at the infinite places
gives differential equations over $\kbar$ for those coefficients, of the same
kind, which are solved recursively. This is Risch's
method~\cite[pp.~209--211 and~216--219]{raab2022}, and it yields his
statement for the parametric equation: one can find $h_1, \dots, h_r \in E$
and a system of linear equations with constant coefficients such that the
solutions are the $z = \sum_j y_j h_j$ with $(c_1, \dots, c_m, y_1, \dots,
y_r)$ a constant solution of the system. We have not reproduced the
expansions.

\paragraph{The diagonal case.} If $E = k(t)[z_0]/(z_0^n - H)$ is a simple
radical extension and $f \in k(t)$, then in the basis $w$
of~\eqref{eq:radical-basis} the equation~\eqref{eq:risch} falls apart.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rde-radical}\standing{proved}{}
Let $E$ be a simple radical extension of $k(t)$ with the basis $w$
of~\eqref{eq:radical-basis} and $Dw_{i+1} = \lambda_i w_{i+1}$. Let $f \in
k(t)$ and $g = \sum_i g_{i+1} w_{i+1}$ with $g_{i+1} \in k(t)$. Then $z = \sum_i
z_{i+1} w_{i+1}$ with $z_{i+1} \in k(t)$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:risch} if and
only if
\[
  Dz_{i+1} + (f + \lambda_i)\, z_{i+1} = g_{i+1} \qquad (0 \le i < n).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$D(z_{i+1} w_{i+1}) = (Dz_{i+1} + \lambda_i z_{i+1})\, w_{i+1}$, and the
$w_{i+1}$ are linearly independent over $k(t)$.
\end{proof}

These are $n$ Risch differential equations over $k(t)$, solved by
\VolI{Chapter~6}. The equations of Theorem~\ref{thm:log-radical}(ii) are the
case $f = 0$, and those after~\eqref{eq:Sexp} are of this form over $k$. When
$f$ itself involves the radical, the system is coupled and the proposition
does not apply.

\section{Bronstein's rational algorithm}\label{sec:rde-rational}

Bronstein's paper of 1991~\cite{bronstein1991} gives ``a new rational
algorithm for solving Risch differential equations over algebraic curves'',
that is, for $E$ a finite extension of $K(x)$ with $K$ a field of constants.
It never computes in an algebraic closure and never expands in series; the
paper reports an implementation in the Scratchpad system. By the abstract, the method also
applies to linear differential equations of higher order with coefficients in
an algebraic extension of the rational functions; and ``in the general
(`mixed function') case, this algorithm finds the denominator of any
solution of the equation''.

So for a curve over constants the Risch equation has a rational algorithm.
For $E$ over $k(t)$ with $t$ a logarithm or an exponential, the rational
method gives the $d$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:rde-denominator}, and what
remains, equation~\eqref{eq:risch-integral} for an integral $Z$, still goes
through the expansions of Section~\ref{sec:rde-solving}. We have read the
abstract and the conclusion of the paper, whose text layer is unusable, and
have not reproduced its algorithm.

\section{What is open}\label{sec:rde-open}

\begin{openproblem}
A rational algorithm for $Dz + f z = g$ in a finite extension of $k(t)$, $t$
a logarithm or an exponential over $k$: one that finds the numerator of a
solution, as well as its denominator, without Puiseux expansions at the
special places.
\end{openproblem}

This is the third of the obstacles listed in the conclusions
of~\cite{bronstein1990}. The report on the lazy Hermite reduction, eight
years later, still names a generalisation of the Hermite reduction to
equations $Dz + fz = g$ in an algebraic extension of $k(t)$ as a direction
for further work~\cite[Conclusions]{bronstein1998lazy}.

\begin{openproblem}
The cancellation case over a nonlinear monomial. In \VolI{\S6.6}, solving
the Risch equation in $k(t)$ when $t$ is a nonlinear monomial, such as a
tangent, can lead to an equation in a nonconstant algebraic extension of
$k(t)$, and Volume~I says of this: ``no algorithms are known for such curves
when $t$ is a nonlinear monomial. Although the techniques of [11, 73] are
probably generalizable to such curves, they would not yield a practical
algorithm in their current form.''
\end{openproblem}

The references [11, 73] there are Bronstein's paper of 1990 and Risch's
report. Everything in this chapter and the last assumed that $t$ is a
logarithm or an exponential; we know of no source that carries the theory to
a nonlinear monomial, and we do not attempt it.

Let $E = \Q(x)[y]/(y^2 - x)$ and consider $Dz + \frac{1}{2x}\,z = 1$. Find the
possible orders of a solution at the place over $x = 0$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:rde-normal}, and solve the equation with
Proposition~\ref{prop:rde-radical}.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
Show that Theorem~\ref{thm:rde-normal} reduces to the statement of
\VolI{\S6.1} when $E = k(t)$.
\end{exercise}

Use Proposition~\ref{prop:rde-radical} to decide whether $\int \sqrt{x}\,
e^{x}\,dx$ is elementary, by writing the integral as $z\,e^x$.
\end{exercise}
